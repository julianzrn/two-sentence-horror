# Candidates — Two-Sentence Horror

Competition: `u-suroto-two-sentence-horror-acea9253`

Requirement (from the official requirements page): A horror story in exactly two sentences, at most 400 characters in total.

8 candidates were drafted; all are original to this entry.

## Candidate 1 ← SELECTED

The recording of my sleep-talking ends with me begging, 'Don't let it answer in my voice again.' Then there's a pause, and I hear myself, calm and awake, say: 'Too late, he's listening now.'

(190 characters)

## Candidate 2

The baby monitor crackled and a woman's voice sang the lullaby I always sing, note for note. I was alone in the kitchen, and my daughter's room has no other door.

(162 characters)

## Candidate 3

Every night the dog stared at the corner of my bedroom and growled at exactly 3 a.m. Last night she stared at my bed instead, whimpering, and backed out of the room.

(165 characters)

## Candidate 4

The surgeon counted the instruments twice, frowned, and counted again. 'We opened you up with eleven,' she said, 'so why are there twelve on the tray?'

(151 characters)

## Candidate 5

My reflection in the elevator mirror was half a second behind me, and I was willing to call that a trick of the light. Then it smiled while I was still deciding whether to be afraid.

(182 characters)

## Candidate 6

The last photo on my phone is of me asleep in my bed, taken from the foot of it. I live alone, and I have never once set a timer.

(129 characters)

## Candidate 7

Grandma's obituary said she passed peacefully in her sleep, surrounded by family. I found the clipping in her attic, dated fourteen years before she died.

(154 characters)

## Candidate 8

The hiker waved from the ridge every morning, so I waved back for a week before the rangers found my campsite. They asked why my own tent had a second set of footprints, fresher than mine, leading away.

(202 characters)

## Why the selected candidate wins

Exactly two sentences, as the gate requires. The first builds the premise — something that answers in your own voice — and the second delivers the scare in the calmest possible register: the thing is already awake, it has been listening, and it is polite about it.
